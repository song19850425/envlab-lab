About this lab

A shell and tube heat exchanger transfers heat between a hot fluid on the shell side and a cold fluid inside the tubes, and the log mean temperature difference (LMTD) is the driving force that sizes it: heat duty equals overall coefficient times area times LMTD. This heat exchanger simulation models steam condensing on the shell side while cold water warms up in the tubes in counter-flow. You can adjust the cold-water flow and the steam temperature, then read the water outlet temperature, the overall heat-transfer coefficient, and the LMTD, and plot measured Nusselt numbers against Reynolds numbers. It is built for chemical engineering students learning LMTD explained from data rather than from a formula alone: doubling the water flow lifts the coefficient, but the outlet temperature falls, and the trade-off is visible in a single run.

Questions students ask

What is LMTD, in plain terms? The log mean temperature difference is the average driving temperature difference along the exchanger, used in Q = K x A x delta-tm.

How do I use this log mean temperature difference calculator? Set the cold-water flow and steam temperature, run the steps, and read the outlet temperature, overall coefficient, and delta-tm from the live instruments.

Why does doubling the water flow lower the outlet temperature? Faster water carries heat away with less time to warm up, even though the overall coefficient rises with flow.

What do the Nu-Re points show? Each run adds one measured point, so you can see how the heat-transfer correlation builds up with Reynolds number.

Red line 1 · Water first, steam second
Sending steam before the cooling water is on means dry firing: tubes overheat and deform, gaskets burn out. Shut down in reverse order: steam off first, then water off.
Red line 2 · Drain condensate first
On steam-line start-up, drain condensate and warm the pipes first. Undrained condensate hit by steam means water hammer — violent enough to shake pipes off their supports.
Red line 3 · Don't swap the hot and cold ends
A shell-and-tube exchanger has a small area and high heat flux, and steam-side pressure exceeds water-side pressure. Wrong end connections or trapped pressure, and the steam-side joint cracks first.
▸ Model and equations (deterministic demo, no random numbers)
Heat balance: Q = W·cp·(t₂ − t₁) cp = 4180 J/(kg·K), t₁ = 20 °C Heat-transfer rate: Q = K·A·Δtm Log mean temperature difference (counter-flow, steam side at constant temperature): Δtm = (Δt₁−Δt₂)/ln(Δt₁/Δt₂), Δt₁ = T−t₁, Δt₂ = T−t₂ Tube-side water (Dittus-Boelter): Nu = 0.023·Re^0.8·Pr^0.4, h = Nu·k/d, K ≈ h (steam-condensing-side resistance is small, neglected) Properties (water at 25 °C): ρ = 997 kg/m³, μ = 8.94×10⁻⁴ Pa·s, k = 0.62 W/(m·K), Pr = 6.0 Solution: t₂ is obtained by bisection iteration from Q(heat balance) = Q(heat transfer) (deterministic solution on this page, no random numbers) Nu measured = (Q/(A·Δtm))·d/k
