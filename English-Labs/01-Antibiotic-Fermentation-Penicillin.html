About this lab

Penicillin fermentation is an antibiotic fermentation process in which the mould Penicillium chrysogenum first grows biomass and only then, in the stationary phase, produces penicillin as a secondary metabolite. Industrial production runs fed-batch: glucose feed sustains the culture, aeration and agitation supply oxygen, and phenylacetic acid is fed as the side-chain precursor. This fed-batch fermentation simulation follows that penicillin production process hour by hour. You can adjust the glucose feed rate, the agitation speed that sets the oxygen-transfer coefficient, and the precursor feed, then watch biomass, dissolved oxygen, and penicillin titre evolve — and see production collapse when dissolved oxygen falls below its critical level, or when excess precursor inhibits the culture. It is built for pharmaceutical and biochemical engineering students learning why feeding strategy, not just the strain, decides the yield.

Questions students ask

Why is penicillin made after growth stops? Penicillin is a secondary metabolite: the culture produces it mainly in the stationary phase, once rapid growth has ended.

What happens if dissolved oxygen falls too low? Below the critical level, production drops sharply, so the run holds oxygen above that line through feeding and agitation.

Can too much precursor hurt the run? Yes — phenylacetic acid feeds the side chain at moderate levels, but above about 0.9 it inhibits production instead of raising it.

When should I harvest? Harvest when the production rate starts to fall; dragging the run into the decline phase adds little product and risks quality.

Red line 1 · Below critical DO, production drops first
Below 1.5 mg/L (about 20% saturation), growth and production rates fall proportionally — penicillin fermentation is aerobic: oxygen shortage hits production first, then the cells. More air without faster agitation still leaves it oxygen-starved; KLa is the combined effect of aeration and agitation.
Red line 2 · More precursor is not better
Phenylacetic acid is the precursor of penicillin G: a moderate feed directs synthesis toward penicillin G; at a feed level of 0.9 or above, excess precursor inhibits growth by 20% and production falls instead of rising. A precursor is a "directing agent", not a nutrient.
Red line 3 · Harvest when the production rate falls
Harvest when the production rate falls — don't drag it into the decline phase. One extra day just burns feed for nothing, and the product may even degrade. The endpoint is set just after the production peak, when the rate has clearly fallen — not "the longer the better".
▸ Model and equations (deterministic demo, no random numbers)
Growth (Monod + oxygen limitation + precursor excess inhibition): μ = μmax·S/(Ks+S)·fO2·fPrec [h⁻¹] μmax=0.20,Ks=0.5 g/L (demo);fO2 = C≥1.5 ? 1 : C/1.5; fPrec = precursor level >= 0.9 ? 0.8 : 1.0 Substrate (fed-batch): dS/dt = F − (μ·X)/Y, Y = 0.5 g/g, F is the feed slider Biomass: dX/dt = μ·X − kd·X (kd = 0.03 h⁻¹, active from t >= 120 h) Product (secondary metabolism: the faster the growth, the lower the specific production rate): qp = qpmax·(1 − μ/μmax)·min(1, C/1.5)·(0.3 + 0.7·precursor level) qpmax = 0.012 g/L/h (demo); dP/dt = qp·X Dissolved oxygen (sub-stepped integration to avoid oscillation): dC/dt = KLa·(C*−C) − qO2·X C* = 7.5 mg/L, critical 1.5 mg/L, qO2 = 15 mg/g/L/h (demo) KLa = 50·vvm + 20 + 30·(rpm/600) [h⁻¹] (aeration + agitation coupled demo equation, vvm = 1.0) Phases: lag (t < 8 h) → log (μ > 0.04) → stationary/production (μ ≈ 0, X not falling) → decline (X falling after t >= 120 h) This page is a deterministic demo: feed, agitation and precursor are adjustable, and the curves are reproducible. Industrial potency is usually quoted in u/mL; this page uses g/L to show orders of magnitude. Strain parameters are typical demo values, not a basis for fermentation process design.

The demo model follows typical penicillin fed-batch fermentation kinetics; real strains, media, and precursor strategies vary widely. For teaching demos only — not a basis for production processes.
