About this lab

Demo: Enzyme kinetics asks a simple question: how fast does an enzyme work at a given substrate concentration? The Michaelis-Menten equation answers it: v = Vmax·[S]/(Km + [S]), where v is the initial rate, Vmax is the rate at saturating substrate, and Km is the substrate concentration at half of Vmax. In this interactive lab you adjust the substrate concentration [S], Vmax and Km, and watch the saturation curve take shape: at low substrate the rate rises almost linearly with [S], and at high substrate it flattens towards Vmax. The same data appear as a Lineweaver-Burk double-reciprocal plot, 1/v against 1/[S], whose intercept is 1/Vmax and whose slope is Km/Vmax. Km measures affinity — smaller means tighter binding — while Vmax scales with enzyme amount, so the two parameters must be reported together, with pH and temperature stated.

Red line 1 · Michaelis-Menten only holds for initial rates
The Michaelis-Menten equation holds only early in the reaction — before product accumulates, with no reverse reaction and no product inhibition. What sampling must measure is the "slope", not the end-point concentration. If the reaction runs long, v drifts off the curve through substrate consumption and product inhibition.
Red line 2 · Km is the inverse of affinity
The smaller Km, the stronger the enzyme's affinity for substrate (the less substrate is needed to reach half of Vmax). Km is an intrinsic enzyme property (dependent on pH/temperature), while Vmax varies with enzyme amount — specific activity = Vmax/[E]; report both parameters with their conditions stated.
Red line 3 · Double-reciprocal plots magnify error
The Lineweaver-Burk line magnifies the error of low-substrate points (large 1/[S]) most — high-substrate points are actually more trustworthy. Modern practice uses nonlinear fitting (direct Michaelis-Menten regression), which is more robust; Lineweaver-Burk suits teaching and quick visual checks. Do not treat extrapolated intercepts as exact values.

Frequently asked questions

What do Vmax and Km mean?
Vmax is the maximum initial rate at saturating substrate; Km is the substrate concentration at half of Vmax, and a smaller Km means stronger enzyme–substrate affinity.

Why must rates be initial rates?
The Michaelis-Menten equation holds only early in the reaction, before product accumulates, the reverse reaction matters, or product inhibition sets in — so measure the slope, not the end point.

How does the Lineweaver-Burk plot give Vmax and Km?
Plotting 1/v against 1/[S] straightens the curve: the intercept on the 1/v axis is 1/Vmax and the slope is Km/Vmax.

Why is the Lineweaver-Burk plot risky for precise work?
It magnifies the error of low-substrate points, which sit far out on the 1/[S] axis, so formal assays should use direct nonlinear fitting of the Michaelis-Menten equation.
