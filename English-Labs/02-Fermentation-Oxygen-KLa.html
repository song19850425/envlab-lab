DO CONTROL · kLa · OXYGEN TRANSFER

Aerobic fermentation is a tug of war between oxygen supply and oxygen demand: aeration rate and agitation speed set kLa (the oxygen-supply capacity), while biomass sets the oxygen uptake rate. Once DO falls below the critical value, cells switch at once to anaerobic metabolism.
dC/dt = kLa(C* − C) − qO2·X · Dissolved oxygen DO · Critical DO 1.5 mg/L.

About this lab

Demo: Aerobic fermentation lives or dies on oxygen transfer. Oxygen must cross from gas bubbles into the liquid, and the volumetric mass-transfer coefficient kLa measures how fast that transfer can happen. In this interactive lab you set the aeration rate (vvm) and the agitation speed (rpm), which together fix kLa, while the oxygen uptake rate qO2·X is set by biomass. Dissolved oxygen then settles at a steady state, C_ss = C* − qO2·X/kLa, where C* is the saturation concentration. If that steady state falls below the critical dissolved oxygen of 1.5 mg/L, cells switch to anaerobic metabolism and by-products accumulate. After any parameter change, dissolved oxygen approaches its new steady state exponentially, with a time constant of 1/kLa, so a larger kLa means a faster response as well as a higher steady state.

Red line 1 · Critical DO is a gate
For most aerobic microbes the critical DO is 1–2 mg/L; below it respiration is limited and by-products accumulate (lactate/ethanol). Controlling DO is not a case of higher is better: too high wastes energy, foams, and supersaturates CO2 — holding 1–2 times the critical value is enough.
Red line 2 · kLa is not a single aeration knob
kLa is set jointly by aeration rate, agitation, antifoam and vessel geometry. Agitation breaks large bubbles into small ones and extends their residence time, often beating simply adding more air. In high-biomass fermentation oxygen demand is huge — adding air without speeding up agitation still leaves the culture oxygen-starved.
Red line 3 · Agitation power is the main cost
Agitation power P ∝ n³d⁵, so doubling speed multiplies power eightfold. In industry the order "DO falls → add air first, then raise speed, and only then touch feeding" is deliberate — every step costs money. DO probes must be calibrated regularly; drift fools the whole control loop.

Frequently asked questions

What is kLa?
The volumetric oxygen-transfer coefficient: it combines the transfer coefficient and the bubble area per volume, so it measures the fermenter's overall capacity to supply oxygen.

How do aeration and agitation change kLa?
More air and faster stirring both raise kLa in this demo — stirring also breaks large bubbles into small ones and lengthens their residence time, which often beats adding air alone.

What is the critical dissolved oxygen?
The level below which respiration becomes oxygen-limited, here 1.5 mg/L. Below it, cells switch to anaerobic metabolism and lactate or ethanol accumulate.

Why does dissolved oxygen respond exponentially after a change?
Because the balance dC/dt = kLa(C* − C) − qO2·X is first-order in C, so the gap to the new steady state shrinks with time constant 1/kLa.
