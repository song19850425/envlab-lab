About this lab

The McCabe-Thiele method is a graphical method for binary distillation: it steps between the equilibrium curve and the operating lines to count the theoretical stages a column needs for a given separation. This interactive McCabe-Thiele diagram applies it to a benzene-toluene system at atmospheric pressure with a saturated-liquid feed. The page draws the equilibrium curve, the rectifying and stripping operating lines, and the q-line, then steps off the staircase from the distillate composition down to the bottoms composition. You can adjust the reflux ratio R, the feed composition xF, and the relative volatility, and watch the stage count, the minimum reflux ratio, and the feed stage — the stage where the staircase crosses the q-line — update at once. It is built for chemical engineering students learning distillation column simulation, and for anyone checking, before any detailed calculation, whether a lower reflux ratio is still workable or pushes the stage count out of reach.

Questions students ask

How do I find the feed stage in a McCabe-Thiele diagram? Step off stages from the top until the staircase crosses the q-line; the stage at the crossing is the feed stage, and the page reports it directly.

What happens when I lower the reflux ratio? The operating lines move closer to the equilibrium curve, so each step gets smaller and the stage count rises — and below the minimum reflux ratio, separation becomes impossible.

What is this interactive McCabe-Thiele diagram used for? It sizes a binary distillation column graphically, giving the theoretical stage count and feed location for a chosen separation.

Does the page model a real column? It models an idealised benzene-toluene system with constant relative volatility, so treat the stage count as a teaching estimate, not a detailed design.

Red line 1 · Minimum reflux ratio
If R falls below Rmin, the operating line touches the equilibrium curve and the theoretical stage count goes to infinity — the column can never meet the separation target. The closer R is to Rmin, the less energy the column uses, but the stage count soars.
Red line 2 · Reboiler dry-out
Heating on with too low a bottoms level dries out the reboiler, cokes it, and can burn through the exchanger tubes. On shutdown, stop heating before stopping the feed.
Red line 3 · Feed thermal condition
Saturated-liquid feed (q=1) gives a vertical q-line; subcooled liquid q>1, two-phase feed 0<q<1, superheated vapor q<0 — move the q-line and the required stage count changes entirely. This experiment fixes saturated-liquid feed.
▸ Model and equations (deterministic demo, no random numbers)
Equilibrium curve: y = αx / [1 + (α−1)x] Rectifying-section operating line: y = (R/(R+1))·x + xD/(R+1) Stripping-section operating line: y = (L̄/(L̄−W))·x − (W·xB)/(L̄−W), L̄ = R·D + q·F Material balance: F·xF = D·xD + W·xB (F=100 kmol/h, xD=0.95, xB=0.05, q=1) Minimum reflux ratio: q-line/equilibrium-curve intersection (xq, yq), Rmin = (xD−yq)/(yq−xq) Theoretical stages: from (xD,xD), alternate horizontal to the operating line and vertical to the equilibrium curve, until x ≤ xB This page is a deterministic graphical construction; α, xF and R are all adjustable, and results match hand construction.

The demo model follows the McCabe-Thiele method for tray distillation; the benzene-toluene relative volatility is the atmospheric-pressure average. For teaching demos only — not a basis for engineering design.
