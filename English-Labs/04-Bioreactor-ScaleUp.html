At 300 rpm a 1 L shake flask runs fine — scale it to 100 L at the same speed and tip speed goes through the roof and power explodes. Scale-up is not proportional enlargement; it is a decision about "which quantity to hold constant".
Geometric similarity λ = (V2/V1)^(1/3) · Constant P/V · Constant tip speed · Constant Re.

About this lab

Demo: Scaling up a bioreactor is not a matter of enlarging everything in proportion. Under geometric similarity, vessel and impeller diameters grow by λ = (V2/V1)^(1/3), but impeller speed must then be chosen by a criterion — and no single criterion preserves everything. In this interactive lab you set the scale-up factor from a 1 L baseline vessel running at 300 rpm, and compare three speeds: constant power per unit volume (P/V), which keeps power draw and oxygen supply similar; constant tip speed, which keeps shear as a floor; and constant Reynolds number, which keeps the flow pattern but drops speed the most. For aerobic fermentation the usual main thread is holding kLa or P/V, because oxygen supply is the limiting factor, followed by step-by-step verification at each scale, from 1 L to 100 L to 1 m³ and beyond.

Red line 1 · No single criterion preserves everything
Constant P/V keeps power draw and shear similar, but tip speed rises; constant tip speed keeps shear, but P/V falls and mixing worsens; constant Re keeps the flow pattern, but speed falls hardest. Scale-up must accept "holding one loses another" and choose by the key limiting factor.
Red line 2 · Oxygen transfer is the usual main thread
Aerobic-fermentation scale-up most often holds kLa or P/V — because the limiting factor is oxygen supply. Real engineering also adds vvm aeration corrections and vessel pressure, calibrating iteratively with the empirical correlation kLa ∝ (P/V)^a·vs^b, not by picking one speed and stopping.
Red line 3 · Scale-up must be verified step by step
Scale up step by step, 1 L → 100 L → 1 m³ → 20 m³, checking DO/temperature/pH/mixing time at each step and tracking by-products with metabolic flux analysis (MFA). Jumping from a shake flask to an industrial vessel in one step leaves the cells' fate to luck.
▸ Model and equations (deterministic demo, no random numbers fitted to results)
Geometric-similarity scale-up: λ = (V2/V1)^(1/3), impeller diameter d2 = λ·d1 Three-criterion speeds (baseline n1 = 300 rpm, small vessel d1): Constant P/V (power per unit volume): n2 = n1·λ^(−2/3) (P ∝ n³d⁵, P/V ∝ n³d²) Constant tip speed: n2 = n1·λ^(−1) (u_tip = π·n·d) Constant Reynolds number: n2 = n1·λ^(−2) (Re ∝ n·d²) Demo: 1 L baseline vessel n1 = 300 rpm → 100 L: λ = 4.64, Constant P/V → 108 rpm; constant tip speed → 65 rpm; constant Re → 14 rpm This page is a deterministic demo: The scale-up factor is adjustable; the three-criterion speeds are reproducible.

Frequently asked questions

What is geometric similarity in scale-up?
It means vessel height, diameter, impeller diameter and impeller type all scale together by λ = (V2/V1)^(1/3), so the large vessel is a true enlargement of the small one.

Why can't one criterion preserve everything?
Constant P/V lets tip speed rise; constant tip speed lets P/V fall and mixing worsen; constant Reynolds number drops speed hardest — holding one quantity always loses another.

Which criterion is usual for aerobic fermentation?
Holding kLa or P/V, because oxygen supply is normally the limiting factor, with aeration rate and vessel pressure corrections added in real engineering.

What does the scale-up factor slider change?
It sets V2/V1, hence λ, and all three criterion speeds are recomputed at once, so you can see how far apart constant P/V, tip-speed and Reynolds speeds drift as scale grows.
