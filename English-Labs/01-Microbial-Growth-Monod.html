BATCH CULTURE · OD600 · MONOD

Inoculated microbes do not explode at once — first they adapt (lag phase), then double exponentially (log phase), then flatten out once the substrate is gone (stationary phase), and finally die off. OD600 records all four acts.
Monod μ = μmax·S/(Ks+S) · OD600 turbidimetry · Four phases · Specific growth rate.

About this lab

Demo: Batch culture is the simplest way to see how microbes grow, and the Monod equation describes its central control: the specific growth rate μ equals μmax·S/(Ks+S), where S is substrate concentration and Ks is the substrate level at half the maximum rate. In this interactive lab you adjust the maximum specific growth rate μmax and the initial substrate S0, then follow OD600 — a turbidimetric proxy for biomass — over 24 hours. The curve passes through four phases: a lag phase while cells adapt, a log phase of exponential doubling, a stationary phase once substrate is exhausted, and a death phase as cells decline. The yield ceiling is Xmax = S0·Y + X0, so substrate supply, not wishful thinking, sets the final biomass. Clicking the curve samples any time point, and the conclusion page reads off the log-phase μ and the yield ceiling.

Red line 1 · OD600 is turbidity, not cell count
OD600 measures light scattering; cell shape, colour and dead cells all interfere. Above OD 1.0 the response leaves linearity, so dilute and re-measure. For exact cell numbers use plate counts or dry weight — OD is only fit for trend monitoring.
Red line 2 · The lag phase is not wasted time
During the lag phase cells adapt to the new environment and synthesise enzyme systems. Inoculating with log-phase seed, using the same medium, and inoculating enough (5–10%) shortens the lag phase — in industrial production the lag phase is costly idle time.
Red line 3 · Measuring μ in the log phase has a precondition
Only when substrate is plentiful (S ≫ Ks) is μ ≈ μmax constant, so that the slope of a semi-log straight line counts. Once substrate limits growth, μ falls at once by Monod kinetics. Scale-up design and feeding strategy both revolve around "which μ range to hold".
▸ Model and equations (deterministic demo, no random numbers fitted to results)
Monod equation: μ = μmax·S/(Ks+S) [h⁻¹], Ks = 0.2 g/L(demo) Growth and substrate consumption (Euler stepping, dt = 0.05 h): dX/dt = μ·X − kd·X (death term, activated after the stationary phase, kd = 0.08 h⁻¹) dS/dt = −(1/Y)·μ·X, Y = 0.5 g/g (biomass yield) OD600 = X·2.2 (turbidimetric calibration, demo) Four phases: lag (t < 1.5 h) → log (μ > 0.2·μmax) → stationary (μ ≈ 0, substrate exhausted) → death (X falls) This page is a deterministic demo: μmax and S0 are adjustable; the curve is reproducible.

Frequently asked questions

What is Monod kinetics?
It is the empirical rule μ = μmax·S/(Ks+S): growth rate rises with substrate and saturates at μmax, with Ks marking the substrate concentration at half that maximum.

Why does the growth curve have four phases?
Cells first adapt (lag), then double exponentially while substrate is plentiful (log), then stop when substrate runs out (stationary), and finally die as nutrients exhaust and metabolites turn toxic (death).

What do the μmax and S0 sliders change?
μmax sets how fast cells can double at best; S0 sets how much substrate is available, and therefore the final biomass ceiling Xmax = S0·Y + X0.

Why is OD600 not a true cell count?
OD600 measures light scattering, so cell shape, colour and dead cells interfere, and readings leave linearity above OD 1.0 — dilute before measuring, and use plate counts or dry weight for exact numbers.
